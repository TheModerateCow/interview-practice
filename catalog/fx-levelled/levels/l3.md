## Level 3: rate history and wallet value

Rates move during the day, and stale rates must not be used. Support wants to quote what a conversion would have credited at an earlier time, and customers want to see what their whole wallet is worth in one currency. Add:

| Method | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `{{quote_at}}(ts, source, target, amount, {{at_ts}}) → int` | What `{{quote}}` would have returned at time `{{at_ts}}` (`{{at_ts}}` is never after `ts`), using the rate in force then. Same `-1` cases as `{{quote}}`, judged at `{{at_ts}}`. |
| `{{total_value}}(ts, {{wallet_id}}, currency) → int` | The wallet's worth in `currency`: its balance in `currency` itself, plus, for every other currency it holds above zero, what `{{quote}}` gives for converting that whole balance into `currency` right now (each one rounded on its own, fee included). `0` for an empty wallet. Returns `-1` if the wallet doesn't exist, `currency` isn't a currency, or some currency it holds has no rate in force into `currency`. |

**The rate in force.** Every `{{set_rate}}` is kept. The rate in force for a pair at time `t` is the one from the latest `{{set_rate}}` for that pair at or before `t` (several on the same timestamp: the last call counts).

**Rates expire.** A rate published at `p` is in force up to and including `p + 99`. From `p + 100` on the pair has no rate, until a newer one is published. This applies to everything that uses a rate: `{{quote}}`, `{{convert}}`, `{{quote_at}}` and `{{total_value}}`.

### Example

```
{{open_wallet}}(1, "ana"); {{deposit}}(2, "ana", "USD", 10000)
{{set_rate}}(10, "USD", "EUR", 900000); {{set_rate}}(50, "USD", "EUR", 950000)
{{quote_at}}(60, "USD", "EUR", 1000, 30)    → 891     (the rate from 10)
{{quote_at}}(61, "USD", "EUR", 1000, 50)    → 941     (940.5 rounds up)
{{quote}}(149, "USD", "EUR", 1000)          → 941
{{convert}}(150, "ana", "USD", "EUR", 1000) → -1      (the rate from 50 expired at 150)
{{deposit}}(151, "ana", "EUR", 100)
{{set_rate}}(160, "USD", "EUR", 1000000)
{{total_value}}(161, "ana", "EUR")          → 10000   (100 EUR + 9900 for the USD)
```
