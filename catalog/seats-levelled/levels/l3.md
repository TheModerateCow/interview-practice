## Level 3: busiest legs on long routes

The airline now runs the desk on its long multi-stop routes, and the operations team wants to know how crowded the cabin gets along any stretch of the route. Add:

| Method | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `{{busiest}}({{from_stop}}, {{to_stop}}) → count or none` | The most seats held on any one leg from stop `{{from_stop}}` to stop `{{to_stop}}` (legs `{{from_stop}}` … `{{to_stop}}-1`). Booked and upgraded passengers hold seats; waitlisted ones don't. *None* if the stretch isn't valid, by the same rule as a trip (`0 <= {{from_stop}} < {{to_stop}} <= n`). |

**Scale.** The desk is now load-tested on very long routes: up to **400 000 legs**, trips and stretches spanning most of the route, and up to **50 000 calls** on one flight. `{{book}}`, `{{cancel}}`, `{{free_seats}}`, `{{request_upgrade}}`, `{{status}}` and `{{busiest}}` must each stay fast however many legs a trip or stretch covers. `{{promote}}` may take time in proportion to the number of waiting requests, but not to the length of their trips. The hidden tests include a busy long route with a time limit.

### Example

```
{{new Flight}}([3, 1, 2, 2, 3])
{{book}}("ana", 0, 3)               → true
{{book}}("ben", 2, 5)               → true
{{request_upgrade}}("cy", 1, 2)     → "waitlisted"   (leg 1 is full)
{{request_upgrade}}("dee", 3, 5)    → "upgraded"
{{busiest}}(0, 5)                   → 2              (legs 2, 3 and 4 each hold 2)
{{busiest}}(0, 2)                   → 1              (cy is only waiting)
{{free_seats}}(3)                   → 0
{{cancel}}("ben")                   → true
{{busiest}}(2, 5)                   → 1
{{busiest}}(4, 4)                   → none
{{busiest}}(0, 6)                   → none
```
