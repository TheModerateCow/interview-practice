## Level 4: the best upgrade plan

Revenue management suspects first come, first served wastes seats: one early request for a long trip can block several shorter ones. Before the flight closes they want to know how many upgrades the cabin could really carry. Add:

| Method | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `{{best_plan}}() → int` | The largest number of current upgrade requests that could all be seated at the same time, each for its whole trip, without any leg holding more than its seats. |

**What counts.** Booked passengers (from `{{book}}`) keep their seats: the plan only uses the seats they leave on each leg. Every current upgrade request is a candidate, whether it is `"upgraded"` or `"waitlisted"` right now; cancelled requests are not. Two candidates may have the same trip.

**It's a what-if.** `{{best_plan}}` changes nothing: every passenger keeps their seat and status, and the waitlist keeps its order.

**Scale.** As in level 3. A flight can hold tens of thousands of requests, most of them for long trips, and revenue management calls `{{best_plan}}` often (dozens of times on one flight); the hidden tests include a large flight with a time limit.

### Example

```
{{new Flight}}([2, 1, 2, 1, 2])
{{request_upgrade}}("ana", 2, 4)    → "upgraded"
{{request_upgrade}}("ben", 3, 5)    → "waitlisted"
{{request_upgrade}}("cy", 2, 3)     → "upgraded"
{{request_upgrade}}("dee", 0, 4)    → "waitlisted"
{{request_upgrade}}("eve", 4, 5)    → "upgraded"
{{request_upgrade}}("fay", 1, 3)    → "waitlisted"
{{promote}}()                       → []
{{best_plan}}()                     → 4      (cy, fay, ben and eve fit together)
{{status}}("ben")                   → "waitlisted"
{{book}}("zed", 4, 5)               → true
{{best_plan}}()                     → 3      (leg 4 now has one seat for the plan)
```

Taking `dee` or `ana` as well as those four would overfill leg 1 or leg 2. Seating the earliest boarder first (`dee`) gets only 3.
