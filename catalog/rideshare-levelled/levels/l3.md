## Level 3: dispatch radius and capacity

Operations sets one **dispatch radius** for the whole corridor: a driver may be sent to a rider only if they are at most that many km apart. Operations also wants to know how many of the waiting riders the idle drivers could serve right now if they were all sent at once. Add:

| Method | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `{{set_radius}}(ts, radius)` | Sets the dispatch radius (`radius >= 0`), replacing any earlier one. Until it's first called there is **no limit**. |
| `{{max_served}}(ts) → int` | The largest number of waiting riders that idle drivers could pick up at the same time: each driver takes at most one rider, each rider gets at most one driver, and every pair is at most the radius apart. With no radius set, distance doesn't matter. It changes nothing. |

**The radius applies to dispatch too.** From now on `{{nearest_driver}}` and `{{dispatch}}` only consider idle drivers at most the radius from the rider (a driver exactly the radius away counts), and return *none* if there aren't any. The rider keeps waiting.

`{{max_served}}` isn't what dispatching riders one by one would achieve: sending each rider their nearest driver can use up the driver another rider needed. The corridor can hold thousands of drivers and riders, and operations calls `{{max_served}}` often, so it has to stay fast.

### Example

```
(d1 idle at 5, d2 at 7; r1 waiting at 6, r2 at 5)
{{max_served}}(5)                             → 2
{{set_radius}}(6, 1)
{{max_served}}(7)                             → 2        (r1 with d2, r2 with d1)
{{nearest_driver}}(8, "r1")                   → "d1"     (both 1 km away: lower marker)
{{add_rider}}(9, "r3", 20)                    → true
{{nearest_driver}}(10, "r3")                  → none     (nobody within 1 km)
{{dispatch}}(11, "r3")                        → none
{{dispatch}}(12, "r1")                        → "d1"
{{max_served}}(13)                            → 0        (r2 at 5 can't reach d2 at 7)
```
