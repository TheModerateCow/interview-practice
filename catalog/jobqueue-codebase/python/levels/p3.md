# Phase 3: the backlog

Stats is live, and the autoscaler reads it. Then a partner's webhook endpoint was down for two hours. Tens of thousands of `webhooks` jobs piled up (some delivered twice, some deferred, a quarter flagged urgent), and when the endpoint came back the workers started draining them while live traffic kept arriving. Every worker loop is lease, then ack or fail; a few jobs fail and are retried, a few workers crash mid-job, ops cancels the odd job by hand, and the autoscaler calls `stats` every few seconds.

The drain started slow and stayed slow: each lease took milliseconds, and the backlog barely moved.

The workload to handle: a backlog of **16,000 jobs**, then **22,400 worker steps** with new jobs arriving along the way. The drain must take **under 2 seconds**. Results (what is leased in what order, what is retried, dead-lettered or cancelled, and the stats) must not change.

Find where the time goes and fix it. Bear in mind that order depends on priority, then on when a job *became ready* (not when it was enqueued), and that a job can leave the ready state without being leased.
