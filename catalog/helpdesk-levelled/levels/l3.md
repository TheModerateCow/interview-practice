## Level 3: response-time targets

The team promises customers a resolution time per priority, and the clock stops while the team waits for the customer to reply. Add:

| Method | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `{{due_at}}(ts, ticket_id) → int` | When the ticket must be resolved by (rules below), as of `ts`. `-1` if the ticket doesn't exist. |
| `{{overdue}}(ts) → list of strings` | The ids of every ticket that is `"open"` or `"in_progress"` and past its due time (`ts > due`): earliest due time first, ties oldest ticket first. |

**Targets.** A ticket's due time is the time it was opened plus its target: `"high"` 60 minutes, `"normal"` 240, `"low"` 1440.

**Waiting on the customer.** A new status, `"waiting"`. Two more moves are allowed in `{{set_status}}`: `"in_progress"` → `"waiting"` and `"waiting"` → `"in_progress"`; every other move into or out of `"waiting"` is refused. While a ticket is waiting its clock is paused: every minute it spends waiting pushes its due time back by one minute (a wait still in progress counts up to `ts`). Waiting tickets are never listed by `{{overdue}}`, but they are unresolved: they stay in their agent's `{{queue}}` and count for `{{auto_assign}}`.

Resolving doesn't change the due time, and reopening doesn't restart the clock: a reopened ticket keeps its due time.

### Example

```
{{add_agent}}(0, "maya")
{{open_ticket}}(0, "acme", "high")        → "t1"    (due at 60)
{{open_ticket}}(10, "globex", "normal")   → "t2"    (due at 250)
{{assign}}(20, "t1", "maya"); {{set_status}}(20, "t1", "in_progress")
{{set_status}}(30, "t1", "waiting")       → true
{{due_at}}(50, "t1")                      → 80      (20 minutes waiting so far)
{{set_status}}(70, "t1", "in_progress")   → true
{{due_at}}(75, "t1")                      → 100     (waited 40 minutes)
{{overdue}}(100)                          → []
{{overdue}}(101)                          → ["t1"]
{{overdue}}(300)                          → ["t1", "t2"]
```
