## Level 3: quotes and market buys

Buyers arrive. A buyer who wants `qty` shares now takes the cheapest shares in the book first, working up the price levels and paying each order's own price. Before buying, a buyer can ask for a quote. Add:

| Method | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `{{quote_buy}}(qty) → cost or none` | What buying `qty` shares right now would cost; *none* if fewer than `qty` shares rest in the book. A quote changes nothing. |
| `{{market_buy}}(qty) → cost` | Buys up to `qty` shares and returns what they cost. Shares the book can't supply are dropped (the buy doesn't rest); an empty book costs `0`. |

**How a buy fills.** Cheapest price level first; within a level, orders in queue order. An order whose shares are all taken is **filled** and leaves the book: it can't be cancelled or amended any more, and its id stays used. The last order a buy touches may be taken only in part: it keeps the rest of its shares **and its place at the front** of its queue.

The book can be deep: tens of thousands of price levels, with quotes and buys arriving between new orders and cancellations.

### Example

```
(a1 101x5, a2 100x3, a3 103x4, a4 101x2 were added in that order)
{{quote_buy}}(6)                   → 603     (3 at 100, then 3 at 101)
{{quote_buy}}(15)                  → none    (14 shares rest)
{{market_buy}}(4)                  → 401     (all of a2, then 1 share of a1)
{{queue_at}}(101)                  → ["a1:4", "a4:2"]
{{best_ask}}()                     → 101
{{market_buy}}(20)                 → 1018    (6 at 101, 4 at 103; the other 10 are dropped)
{{best_ask}}()                     → none
{{cancel_order}}("a2")             → false   (filled)
{{market_buy}}(1)                  → 0
```
