## Level 2: rankings

The bank's fraud and marketing teams want to see who moves the most money out, and who holds the most. Add:

| Method | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `TopSpenders(ts int, n int) []string` | Up to `n` open accounts as `"id:spent"`, most spent first; ties by id ascending. An account's **spending** is the total of its successful withdrawals plus the transfers it **sent**. Deposits and transfers received don't count, and neither does anything that failed. Accounts that spent nothing are included (with `0`) if there's room. |
| `AccountsAbove(ts int, min int) []string` | Every open account whose balance is **at least** `min`, as `"id:balance"`, largest balance first; ties by id ascending. |

Ids sort in plain Go string order. Return an empty, non-nil slice when nothing matches (or `n` is 0).

### Example

```
(acc-a, acc-b and acc-c are open, each with 1000 deposited)
Withdraw(7, "acc-a", 300)                  -> 700, true
Transfer(8, "acc-c", "acc-a", 300)         -> 700, true
Withdraw(9, "acc-b", 50)                   -> 950, true
TopSpenders(10, 2)                         -> ["acc-a:300", "acc-c:300"]
TopSpenders(11, 5)                         -> ["acc-a:300", "acc-c:300", "acc-b:50"]
AccountsAbove(12, 950)                     -> ["acc-a:1000", "acc-b:950"]
```
